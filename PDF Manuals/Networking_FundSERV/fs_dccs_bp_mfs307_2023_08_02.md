INTRODUCTION 
Overview 
This guide offers a “how to” reference guide for the following parties when developing internal connections to   the 
[[NSCC]] interface: 
• Bank trusts 
• TPAs 
• [[Broker]]/dealers 
• Insurance [[Companies]] 
• [[Mutual Funds]] 
 
The guide offers both “preferred” and “alternative” processing suggestions to reduce potential misinterpretation of 
the [[NSCC]] record layouts. “Preferred” methods are just that. “Alternative” methods are workarounds of the 
“preferred” method. 
When followed, these guidelines enable successful implementation of your internal [[NSCC]] Defined Contribution 
Interface. 
The [[NSCC]] Defined Contribution Interface is the result of efforts of the ICI Defined Contribution Committee. The 
committee, working closely with [[NSCC]], developed the enhancements necessary to the Fund/SERV
® and 
Networking systems, to support defined contribution business. It enables the TPA/Settling Entity relationship to 
function within Fund/SERV® and Networking. 
The fund, Settling Entity, insurance [[Companies]], and TPA can share the following information exchanged through 
Fund/SERV® and Networking: 
• Trade information 
• Account activity 
• Position balances 
• Dividends information 
 
[[NSCC]] developed the Defined Contribution Processing Guidelines. These guidelines facilitate the exchange of 
information that flows between these parties in the form of Fund/SERV® and Networking data: 
• Bank trust [[Companies]] 
• Third-party administrators (TPAs) 
• [[Broker]]/dealers 
• Insurance [[Companies]] 
• Mutual fund [[Companies]]

GETTING STARTED 
[[NSCC]] Mutual Fund Services 
The National [[securities]] Clearing Corporation ([[NSCC]]) provides the mutual fund industry with a centralized and 
standardized solution. This solution facilitates the processing of mutual fund transaction and the exchange of  
account-related information in an automated environment. Fund/SERV® and Networking systems are available for 
the following clients: 
• Third Party Administrators (TPAs) 
• Trustees/[[Custodians]] 
• Insurance [[Companies]] 
• [[Mutual Funds]] 
 
These parties can utilize these services with mutual fund prices and daily [[Dividend]] rates from [[NSCC]]’s Mutual 
Fund Profile Services (MFPS). The parties were able to utilize these services for the processing of defined 
contribution transactions. 
The following are brief descriptions of the [[NSCC]]’s Mutual Fund Services: 
• Fund/SERV®, started in 1986, is the industry standard for processing and settling mutual fund transactions 
through automated, standardized formats, and a centralized platform. The following parties  can complete 
order entry purchases, exchanges, and redemptions, as well as confirmations, registrations, and money 
[[settlement]]: 
o Fund [[Companies]] 
o Banks and trust [[Companies]] 
o Third-party administrators (TPAs) 
o [[Broker]]/dealers 
o Other distribution firms 
• [[NSCC]] launched a complementary system, Networking, in 1988. Networking is the industry standard for 
account reconciliation and [[Dividend]] processing. Using Networking, fund [[Companies]], [[Broker]]/dealers, and 
other distribution firms can exchange and reconcile all customer account-level information. They can 
exchange and reconcile this information either in an omnibus or non-omnibus environment. 
• This exchange and reconciliation process allows identical information to appear on all parties’ records.  This 
data includes [[settlement]] of cash dividends and capital gains distributions, reporting of direct account activity , 
periodic positions, and year-end reporting for tax purposes. 
• Exchange Traded Fund ([[ETF]]) Processing automates the creation and redemption of [[ETF]] [[securities]].   
[[NSCC]] reports, clears, and settles ETFs and their underlying [[securities]] through its creation/ redemption 
process. [[ETF]] processing also includes a nightly distribution of domestic, foreign, and fixed income  portfolio 
information.

Timeline Sample 
The table that follows is a sample life cycle for processing of defined contribution trades through [[NSCC]]. All times  
are approximate EST for the sample. 
DAY1 (  T) • Until market close: Trades are gathered 
• 4-9 PM: Fund prices are received 
• 9 PM: Cycles for trade aggregation are run 
• <12 AM: Orders (001) transmitted to [[NSCC]] 
DAY2 (T+1) • 2 AM: As-of orders (004) transmitted to [[NSCC]] 
• 2 AM: [[NSCC]] begins transmitting trades to Funds as they are received 
• 3-9 AM: Funds edit trades and confirm or reject back to [[NSCC]]* 
• 2-11 AM: Corrections or firm deletes are available for submission** 
• 11 AM: [[NSCC]] creates [[settlement]] records for confirmed trades (005 or 025) 
• 11:15 AM: Same Day [[settlement]] File transmitted to relevant parties from [[NSCC]] 
• @ 4 PM: Money movement occurs 
• 10 PM: Funds apply trades to account/plan in nightly cycle 
DAY3 ( T+2) • < 9 AM: Fund sends Networking Activity Files to [[NSCC]] 
• > 9 AM: [[NSCC]] transmits Networking Activity File to relevant parties 
• 11 AM: [[NSCC]] creates [[settlement]] records for confirmed trades (005 or 025) that did not make 
previous day’s window and that a party did not cancel 
* Contact individual Fund for cycle cutoff times. 
** Contact individual Fund for support of this function with same day [[settlement]].

Tri-Party Trading

Fund/SERV® transactions. Submission of these transactions obligates the bank/trust company, insurance

• Insurance [[Companies]]: refer to Insurance Company Tri-Party trading for further information. 
Dual-Party Trading 
This scenario assumes that a bank/trust company, [[Broker]]/dealer, or insurance company, acting as record  keeper

• [[Broker]]/dealers: refer [[Broker]]/dealer Dual-Party trading for further information.

[[settlement]] Process 
[[settlement]] of Fund/SERV® trades occurs as a net debit or credit by [[participant]] Number as part of [[NSCC]]’s net  
[[settlement]] process. [[NSCC]] combines dollar amounts owed/due as the result of Fund/SERV ®, Networking, and 
Commission [[settlement]] activity as part of the [[settlement]] process. Remember that [[NSCC]] TPA members [[DO]] not  
settle with [[NSCC]]. They appoint a Settling Entity who is responsible for settling all confirmed transactions on  their 
behalf. 
[[NSCC]] charges all participants for the use of its products and services on the seventh business day of each  
month. These charges appear as a line item on the [[NSCC]] [[settlement]] file (02132518). Charges are net with all  
other debits/credits for that day’s [[settlement]]. [[NSCC]] also bills TPAs on the seventh business day of the month,  
collecting fees through ACH.

Account Scrub Process 
The account scrubbing process can begin after completion of signatures for all necessary agreements. The term 
“scrubbing” comes from the process of identifying dealer accounts before Networking. Because fund [[Companies]] 
held accounts on their books, dealers needed a method of identifying existing fund accounts to solicit for 
Networking. The process required funds review, or “scrub” their books to find accounts belonging to a dealer. 
Traditionally, fund [[Companies]] used the dealer number maintained on their books to identify  dealer accounts. 
Defined contribution processing reverses the scrubbing process. Most fund [[Companies]] did not maintain a 
bank/trust, insurance company, or TPA identifier before Defined Contribution Networking. Therefore, it is the fund  
that identifies accounts for a given Settling Entity and/or TPA. 
• Identification of defined contribution accounts for a given bank/trust company, insurance company, and/or  
TPA varies by fund company. The tax identification number (TIN) of the firm or the plan tax identification 
number (TIN) are methods of identification. [[NSCC]] expects funds to pass that information back to the firm. 
• Funds have to ensure that customer accounts on their internal systems have the correct trading/settling 
relationship for the firm and TPA. Funds also have to know if the TPA and Settling Entity are to receive 
Networking files. 
• Settling agents have varying abilities to use the [[settlement]] Date Override feature. As a result, funds  
sometimes have to update their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles at the [[NSCC]]. This update enables the Settling Entity 
to allow for T+1 [[settlement]]. 
 
Note 
Completion of the scrubbing process before utilizing the [[NSCC]] interface is critical to the success of your conversion. Also note 
that each fund company can have more requirements aside from the ones listed previously. Contact your various fund 
[[Companies]] before beginning the scrubbing process. 
Field Definitions 
The following field definitions relate to Defined Contribution processing within the Fund/SERV ® and Networking 
systems. However, the list is not an inclusive one. Refer to the Fund/SERV® Technical Guide - Chapter 2: 
(Fund/SERV Capabilities) in the Fund/SERV® section and Networking manuals in the Networking section (login 
required) for definitions of all required and optional fields. 
Fields Definitions 
Originator Type This optional Fund/SERV® field (required if a TPA number is present) indicates the originating entity 
of a transaction. Bank/trust [[Companies]], insurance [[Companies]], and [[Broker]]/dealers use a value of 
[[Broker]]/bank/trust/clearing agent [B] in this field. Third party administrators use a value of TPA [T] 
in this field. 
System Code This required Networking field indicates the originating entity of a transaction. Bank/trust [[Companies]], 
insurance [[Companies]], and [[Broker]]/dealers use a value of [[Broker]]/bank/trust/clearing agent [B] in this 
field. Third party administrators use a value of TPA [T] in this field. 
Firm Number This required field is the [[NSCC]] [[participant]] number assigned to the entity responsible for trade 
[[settlement]] (that is, bank/trust, [[Broker]]/dealer, or insurance company). 
[[settlement]] Override 
Reason Code 
This optional conditional field is mandatory if the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date is present. This field

Old Money versus New Money 
Old money and new money are terms that pertain to purchase transactions. Essentially, old money trades  relate 
to non-commissionable dollars, meaning dollars already paid a commission and thus not subject to pay  more 
commission. New money trades relate to commissionable dollars, meaning dollars that have yet to pay a 
commission, and thus subject to pay a commission. 
The NAV Reason Code field on a Fund/SERV® purchase distinguishes old and new money purchases. Use a 
value of new money [7] commissionable defined contribution plan to represent commissionable money  
purchases. Use a value of old money [8] non-commissionable defined contribution plan to represent non- 
commissionable money purchases. 
Ideally, all brokers/dealers, banks/trusts, insurance [[Companies]], and record keepers track commissionable versus 
non-commissionable monies at the fund complex level. They track these monies in accordance with the pricing 
policies of each fund family. 
The next table illustrates some examples of the differences between old money and new money transactions and 
is not all-inclusive. Consult individually with your fund [[Companies]] to determine their parameters.

Old Money (Non-Commissionable) New Money (Commissionable) 
• Fund-to-fund exchanges1 
• Reallocations 
• Loan repayments 
• Contributions 
• [[Stock]] Transfers2

Defined contribution transactions typically settle on T+1. There are two methods for settling defined contribution 
activity on T+1, [[settlement]] Date Override and Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle. 
[[settlement]] Date Override 
This method is the “preferred” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution transactions. 
[[NSCC]] populates the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the Fund/SERV® Order (001) record. It populates the 
record with the expected trade [[settlement]] date according to the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile. Most load fund 
[[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+2. Therefore, orders submitted with a 
trade date of 1/12/ YYYY in most cases contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 1/14/YYYY. Before you set 
them for [[settlement]], As-Of trades require confirmation from the fund. Therefore, [[NSCC]] does not populate the 
Anticipated [[settlement]] Date on As-Of Order (004) records. Alternatively, most no-load fund [[Companies]] have their 
Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+1. 
The [[settlement]] Date Override feature allows firms to disregard, or “override”, the normal fund [[settlement]] cycle.  
Firms override the normal [[settlement]] fund cycle by providing an alternate [[settlement]] date in the Anticipated  
[[settlement]] Date field. For example, defined contribution activity generally settles on T+1.  Firms can override the 
fund’s normal [[settlement]] cycle. Firms can override this [[settlement]] cycle by populating the Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date field with the T+1 date for [[settlement]] on all defined contribution activity. Therefore, defined 
contribution orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY would contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 
1/13/YYYY. 
[[NSCC]] does not populate, delete, or override the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the following records that 
contain a value provided by the submitting firm: 
• Order (001) 
• As-Of Order (004) 
 
Instead, for valid [[settlement]] dates, [[NSCC]] accepts the value provided as the correct [[settlement]] date for the 
transaction. Fund [[Companies]] are responsible confirming or rejecting the trade accordingly. 
Note 
[[NSCC]] requires a value in [[settlement]] Override Reason Code field on all trades that contain an override Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date. Firms can use a value of 401K[02]. 
 
Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle 
This method is the “alternative” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution transactions. 
[[NSCC]] typically schedules trades for [[settlement]] based on the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile. Fund [[Companies]] 
can establish a primary [[settlement]] cycle for each Security Issue [[ID]]. Most load fund [[Companies]] elect to have  their 
profiles set to settle trades on T+2. Alternatively, most no-load fund [[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] 
profiles set to settle activity on T+1. 
[[NSCC]] also allows fund [[Companies]] to establish different, or “alternate”, [[settlement]] cycles by firm. For example,  
firms that exclusively settle bank/trust or defined contribution activity can always settle their activity on T+1. To [[DO]] 
so, fund [[Companies]] can change their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles. The change profiles reflect that all activity  for a 
given firm settles on T+1 instead of the normal T+2. 
[[NSCC]] populates the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the Fund/SERV® Order (001) record. The field 
contains the expected trade [[settlement]] date according to the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile. If the fund has  
established an alternate [[settlement]] cycle for a firm, this field reflects the alternate [[settlement]] cycle. For  example, 
orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY generally contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date   of 
1/14/YYYY. Orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY for firms with an alternate [[settlement]] cycle of  T+1 
contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 1/13/YYYY.

[[settlement]] 
[[Broker]]/dealers acting in all scenarios (Tri-Party, Dual-Party) are responsible for the [[settlement]] of all transactions 
submitted through Fund/SERV®. The system transmits transactions scheduled for [[settlement]] to both the 
[[Broker]]/dealer and to the fund company as Fund/SERV® Settling Trade Detail (070) records. [[NSCC]] forwards these 
records at approximately 11:30 A.M. ET for [[settlement]] later that day. [[NSCC]] transmits one  Settling Trade Detail 
(070) record for all transactions scheduled for [[settlement]]. Money movement between  [[NSCC]] and the [[participant]] 
settling banks occurs sometime after 4:00 P.M. ET. 
[[NSCC]] anticipates [[settlement]] of all defined contribution transactions on T+1. There are two methods for settling 
defined contribution activity on T+1, [[settlement]] Date Override and Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle. 
[[settlement]] Date Override 
This method is the “preferred” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution transactions. 
[[NSCC]] populates the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the Fund/SERV® Order (001) record. [[NSCC]] 
populates the field with the expected trade [[settlement]] date according to the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile.  Most 
load fund [[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+ 2. Therefore, orders submitted 
with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY in most cases contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of  1/14/YYYY. 
Alternatively, most no-load fund [[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+1. 
As-of trades require confirmation from the fund before you can set them for [[settlement]]. As a result, [[NSCC]] does  
not populate the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date on As-Of Order (004) records. 
The [[settlement]] Date Override feature allows firms to disregard, or “override”, the normal fund [[settlement]] cycle.  
Firms can override the normal fund [[settlement]] cycle by providing an alternate [[settlement]] date in the Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date field. For example, [[NSCC]] generally expects defined contribution activity to settle on T+1. Firms 
override funds’ normal [[settlement]] cycles by populating the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field  with the T+1 date 
for [[settlement]] on all defined contribution activity. In this scenario, defined contribution orders  submitted with a 
trade date of 1/12/YYYY contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 1/13/YYYY. 
[[NSCC]] does not populate, delete, or override the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on all Order (001) or As-Of 
Order (004) records. [[NSCC]] does not perform this population/deletion/override on records that contain a value  
provided by the submitting firm in this field. Instead, for valid [[settlement]] dates, [[NSCC]] accepts the value  provided 
as the correct [[settlement]] date for the transaction. Fund [[Companies]] are responsible for confirming or  rejecting the 
trade accordingly. 
Note 
[[NSCC]] requires a value in the [[settlement]] Override Reason Code field on all trades that contain an overridden Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date. Firms always use a value of 401K[02].

[[settlement]] Date Override 
This method is the “preferred” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution transactions. 
[[NSCC]] populates the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the Fund/SERV® Order (001) record with the 
expected trade [[settlement]] date. It populates the field according to the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile. Most  load 
fund [[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+ 2. Therefore, orders submitted with 
a trade date of 1/12/ YYYY in most cases contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 1/14/YYYY. Before setting 
them for [[settlement]], As-Of trades require confirmation from the fund. As a result, [[NSCC]] does not populate the 
Anticipated [[settlement]] Date on As-Of Order (004) records. Alternatively, most no-load fund [[Companies]] set their 
Security Issue [[ID]] profiles to settle activity on T+1. 
The [[settlement]] Date Override feature allows firms to disregard, or “override”, the normal fund [[settlement]] cycle.  
Firms can override the normal [[settlement]] cycle by providing an alternate [[settlement]] date in the Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date field. For example, [[NSCC]] generally expects defined contribution activity to settle on T+1. As  
such, firms can override the fund’s normal [[settlement]] cycle. Firms can override the cycle by populating the  
Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field with the T+1 date for [[settlement]] on all defined contribution activity. 
Therefore, defined contribution orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY contain an Anticipated  
[[settlement]] Date of 1/13/YYYY. 
[[NSCC]] does not populate, delete, or override the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on Order (001) or As-Of 
Order (004) records. [[NSCC]] does not perform this population/deletion/override on records that contain a value 
provided by the submitting firm in this field. Instead, for valid [[settlement]] dates, [[NSCC]] accepts the value provided 
as the correct [[settlement]] date for the transaction 
Note 
[[NSCC]] requires a value in the [[settlement]] Override Reason Code field on all trades that contain an overridden Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date. Firms use a value of 401K[02]. 
 
Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle 
This method is the “alternative” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution transactions. 
[[NSCC]] typically schedules trades for [[settlement]] based on the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile. [[NSCC]] allows  fund 
[[Companies]] to establish a primary [[settlement]] cycle for each Security Issue [[ID]]. Most load fund [[Companies]] elect to 
have their profiles set to settle trades on T+2. Alternatively, most no-load fund [[Companies]] have their Security 
Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+1. 
[[NSCC]] also allows fund [[Companies]] to establish different, or “alternate”, [[settlement]] cycles by firm. For example,  
firms that exclusively settle bank/trust or defined contribution activity can always settle their activity on T+1. To 
accomplish this, fund [[Companies]] can change their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles. All activities on the changed  profile 
reflect that a given firm settles on T+1 instead of the normal T+2. 
[[NSCC]] populates the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the Fund/SERV® Order (001) record. [[NSCC]] 
populates the record with the expected trade [[settlement]] date, according to the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile.  If 
the fund has established an alternate [[settlement]] cycle for a firm, this field reflects the alternative cycle. For  
instance, orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY in most cases contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] 
Date of 1/14/YYYY. Orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY for firms with an alternate [[settlement]]  cycle 
of T+1 contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 1/13/YYYY. 
Because As-Of trades require confirmation from the fund setting them for [[settlement]], [[NSCC]] does not populate the 
Anticipated [[settlement]] Date on As-Of Order (004) records. Therefore, the firm typically populates the 
Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on Fund/SERV® As-Of Order (004) records. Some fund [[Companies]] are unable 
to support T+1 [[settlement]] on As-Of trades using the alternate [[settlement]] cycle feature.

Old Money versus New Money 
Old money and new money are terms that pertain to purchase transactions. Essentially, old money trades  relate 
to non-commissionable dollars, meaning dollars that have already paid a commission and thus not  subject to pay 
more commission. New money trades relate to commissionable dollars, meaning dollars  that have yet to pay a 
commission, and thus subject to pay a commission. 
The NAV Reason Code field on a Fund/SERV® purchase distinguishes old and new money purchases. Use a 
value of new money [7] commissionable defined contribution plan to represent commissionable money  
purchases. Use a value of old money [8] non-commissionable defined contribution plan to represent non- 
commissionable money purchases. 
Ideally, all brokers/dealers, banks/trusts, insurance [[Companies]], and record keepers track, commissionable  versus 
non-commissionable monies at the fund complex level in accordance with the pricing policies of each fund family. 
The next table illustrates some examples of the differences between old money and new money transactions. The 
table is not all-inclusive. Consult individually with your fund [[Companies]] to determine their parameters. 
Old Money versus New Money 
 
Old Money (Non-Commissionable) New Money (Commissionable) 
• Fund to fund exchanges1 
• Reallocations 
• Loan repayments 
• Contributions 
• [[Stock]] Transfers2 
 
Given the differences between old money and new money purchases, [[NSCC]] prefers submission of defined 
contribution plan activity as follows: 
1. One (1) purchase per plan per fund representing new money into the plan defined previously. These orders  
contain an NAV Reason Code of new money [7] commissionable defined contribution plan,  representing 
commissionable dollars. 
2. One (1) purchase per plan per fund representing old money within the plan defined previously. These orders 
contain an NAV Reason Code of old money [8] non-commissionable defined contribution plan, representing 
non-commissionable dollars. 
3. One (1) redemption per plan per fund representing exchanges and liquidations from the plan within  a given 
fund complex. 
 
Consult individually with your fund [[Companies]] before developing this functionality. 
Firm Exits 
TPAs can cancel or “exit” any transactions before [[settlement]]. TPAs can submit Fund/SERV ® Firm Exit (139) 
records to remove transactions from the [[settlement]] process. The Settling Entity can also submit Firm Exit (139)  
transactions. Submit Firm Exit (139) transactions to [[NSCC]] before cut-off times for [[settlement]]. [[securities]] with USN 
Currency Codes would need to submit a  Firm Exit (139) by 8:00pm ET the day prior to [[settlement]].  [[securities]] 
with USD Currency Codes would need to submit by 11:00am ET on the day of [[settlement]].

Commissions 
The “preferred” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution activity submitted by the TPA is for gross dollars. The 
fund pays any trade commission monies due to the [[Broker]] of Record on the account. 
TPAs also listed as the [[Broker]] of Record on the account can settle transactions for net dollars. They can also 
keep any commission monies up-front. 
[[settlement]] 
TPAs acting as the plan record keeper and obligating a Settling Entity for [[settlement]] [[DO]] not participate in the  
money [[settlement]] process. [[settlement]] of all TPA-initiated transactions occurs between the Settling Entity and 
[[NSCC]], and between [[NSCC]] and the fund company. 
[[NSCC]] transmits transactions scheduled for [[settlement]] to both the Settling Entity and to the fund company as  
Fund/SERV® Settling Trade Detail (070) records. 
[[NSCC]] forwards these records at approximately 11:30 A.M. ET for [[settlement]] later that day. [[NSCC]] transmits  one 
Settling Trade Detail (070) record for all transactions scheduled for [[settlement]]. Money movement between [[NSCC]] 
and the [[participant]] settling banks occurs sometime after 4:00 P.M. ET. 
[[NSCC]] anticipates [[settlement]] of all defined contribution transactions on T+1. There are two methods for settling 
defined contribution activity on T+1. 
[[settlement]] Date Override 
This method is the “preferred” method of [[settlement]] for defined contribution transactions. 
[[NSCC]] populates the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on the Fund/SERV® Order (001) record with the 
expected trade [[settlement]] date. [[NSCC]] populates the record according to the fund’s Security Issue [[ID]] profile.  
Most load fund [[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+ 2. Therefore, orders 
submitted with a trade date of 1/12/ YYYY in most cases, contain an Anticipated [[settlement]] Date of 1/14/YYYY. 
Before setting them for [[settlement]], As-Of trades require confirmation from the fund. As a result, [[NSCC]] does not 
populate the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date on As-Of Order (004) records. Alternatively, most no-load fund 
[[Companies]] have their Security Issue [[ID]] profiles set to settle activity on T+1. 
This feature allows firms to disregard, or “override”, the normal fund [[settlement]] cycle. They can override this  cycle 
by providing an alternate [[settlement]] date in the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field. For example, [[NSCC]] generally 
expects [[settlement]] of defined contribution activity on T+1. As such, firms can override the fund’s  normal 
[[settlement]] cycle. They override the cycle by populating the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field with the T+1 date 
for [[settlement]] on all defined contribution activity. 
Therefore, defined contribution orders submitted with a trade date of 1/12/YYYY would contain an Anticipated 
[[settlement]] Date of 1/13/YYYY. 
[[NSCC]] does not populate, delete, or override the Anticipated [[settlement]] Date field on all Order (001) or As-Of 
Order (004) records. [[NSCC]] does not perform this population/deletion/override on records that contain a value  
provided by the submitting firm in this field. Instead, [[NSCC]] accepts the value provided as the correct [[settlement]] 
date for the transaction, as long as it is a valid [[settlement]] date. Fund [[Companies]] are responsible for  confirming or 
rejecting the trade accordingly.

Networking 
The TPA Number field drives networking data to the TPA on the Networking Activity (F55), [[Dividend]] (F61/62),  and 
Position (F57) records. The TPAs membership number in this field instructs [[NSCC]] to send Networking records to 
both the TPA and the corresponding Settling Entity. The following provides a brief description of  each of the 
Networking files 
Activity 
The Networking Activity File is a fund-generated file. Funds use the file to relay activity that the TPA does not 
initiate, such as purchases, redemptions, cancellations, systematic exchanges. The file contains detailed 
transaction information and the ending share balance for the account. For reconciliation purposes, Funds  should 
send an F55 Activity record indicating the closing share balance when there is a share balance change  on the 
account. 
The Activity Detail Record #3 is a supplemental record that contains the Cash Disbursement Indicator field. 
This field contains both settling and non-settling values. The Cash Disbursement Indicator field indicates: 
• Non-[[settlement]] for values 1 through 5 
• [[settlement]] for values 0 [Cash to Firm] or 6 [Cash to Trust].
