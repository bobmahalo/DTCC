Order Process 
Introduction 
This topic contains information on the order process used to buy, sell, or exchange [[Mutual Funds]].  
Definition 
An order is a firm's instruction to buy, sell, or exchange mutual fund [[shares]]. Firms begin the order process by 
submitting transaction data to [[NSCC]] on the Trade Date for Regular orders or after trade date for As-Of Orders.  
Types of Orders 
Fund/SERV supports several different types of orders; each is described in the table below.  
Order Type  Description  
Regular  Regular orders are orders originated by a firm for the purchase or redemption of [[Mutual Funds]], 
which are entered into Fund/SERV on the Trade Date.  
As-Of  As-Of Orders are mutual fund purchases or redemptions that are processed after the Trade 
Date.  
The historical Net [[Asset]] Value (NAV) for the "as-of" date is checked by the fund so that the 
proper share/dollar confirmation can be generated. 
(When a fund client completes the Fund/SERV Security Issue [[ID]] Add or Modify form with the 
As of Processing Range, the range entered applies only to Record type 004). 
Exchange  Exchange orders allow a fund [[investor]] to exchange fund [[shares]] from a fund within a fund 
group for another fund within the same family of funds.  
Fund Originated  Fund originated orders allow a fund to enter an order on behalf of a firm. A fund originated 
order includes order data and confirmation data.  
Fund Originated As-Of  Fund originated as-of orders allow a fund to enter an order on behalf of a firm after the Trade 
Date.  
As-of Exchange A firm submits an Exchange Order after trade date requesting to [[Liquidate]] one issue and 
subsequently buy another issue with the proceeds.  Typically, both issues are within the same 
family of funds.

Submitting an Order 
The Fund/SERV order cycle begins with a firm submitting a file by the Fund/SERV system.  
Firms must submit orders with their own firm-generated Control Numbers. Orders may be submitted for any 
security type including:

26 No Load mutual fund

48 Interval Fund 
55 GIC (Guaranteed Investment Contract)

57 Separate Account

fund (security type 48) processing and future dating redemption orders.

Order Schedule  
Generally, an order is submitted on Trade Date. As-Of Orders may be submitted from Trade Date+1 to Trade 
Date+N (the Security and [[Member]] Profiles define N). Market fluctuations that cause gains or losses are the 
responsibility of each client.  
Note:  
All orders except Money Markets may be expressed in dollars or [[shares]].  
Money Market Orders  
Money Market purchase orders submitted prior to the [[settlement]] cut-off of the security will settle on Trade 
Date.  
Note: 
Registration must accompany orders (except for Defined Contribution Clearance & [[settlement]] orders)  
Money Market orders can only be expressed in dollars. 
[[NSCC]] sends Firms the order detail with a new Anticipated [[settlement]] Date for any Money Market Orders received after the 
Security’s [[settlement]] cut-off. 
Note: 
[[DTCC]] eligible [[securities]] are defined in the [[DTCC]] Security Master - a stand-alone system for transacting with [[DTCC]].  
[[Mutual Funds]] must complete a Fund/SERV Security Issue [[ID]] Form for each security they wish to make eligible for trading 
on the Fund/SERV system.  This setup allows funds to tailor how they trade [[securities]] on Fund/SERV. 
Some money market funds are coded with a "Security Type" indicator of "24=Money Market Fund", however most money 
market funds are coded with an indicator of "26=No Load Fund".  This document primarily focuses on the money market 
funds coded with the indicator of "24".  For information on money market funds coded with the indicator of "26", please refer 
to the "Money Market Fund Reform User Guide and Best Practices" document. 
Effective September 26, 2016, enhancements to the Fund/SERV system (please refer to Important Notice a8159) to 
address amendments adopted by the [[securities]] Exchange Commission ([[SEC]]) with regards to certain Money Market funds 
were implemented.  For details and best practices on these amendments, which include the implementation of [[Liquidity]] fee 
and redemption gate functionality, please refer to the "Money Market Fund Reform User Guide and Best Practices" 
document published by [[DTCC]]. 
Redemptions  
All redemption orders including Money Market orders must be confirmed by the Fund prior to [[settlement]] 
because full liquidations and withholding amounts must be calculated by the Fund.  
Redemptions will settle according to their [[settlement]] cycle, if confirmed.  
USD/USN Currency Type Considerations 
Definition 
The USN/USD indicators are currency types that determine the timing of when the [[settlement]] report is 
generated and distributed. 
 Both USD and USN settle in US Dollars.

Currency Types 
1. Currency type is required at the Client Level. 
2. Currency type is now required at the Security Issue [[ID]] level. 
3. The system will determine if currency type is provided at the Security Issue [[ID]] level, if not, then the client  
level currency type will be applied to the transaction. 
4. The [[Member]] and Security Profile Forms  (not to be confused with the MFPS (Mutual Fund Profile Service) 
allow funds to set their processing capabilities, Security Issue IDs, Security Types, Currency Types and 
[[settlement]] Cycles. 
Security Issue [[ID]] [[settlement]] Cycles 
• Security Issue [[ID]] [[settlement]] Cycles are typically setup based on the information provided by the fund on 
the Security Issue [[ID]] Add/Modify and Fund/SERV [[Member]]  Profile Forms 
• Security Issue [[ID]] Add/Modify and Fund/SERV [[Member]] Modify Profile Forms can be accessed via the 
MyDTCC portal at https://portal.[[DTCC]].com  
 
The following [[settlement]] cycles are specified by [[NSCC]] clients for each Security Issue [[ID]] on the above 
referenced form: 
• Exchange [[settlement]] cycle - number of days from confirmation date (T+X, where T=confirmation date) 
• Order [[settlement]] Cycle - number of Days from Trade Date (T+X, where T=Trade Date) 
• As of Orders [[settlement]] Cycle - number of Days from Confirmation Date (T+X, where T=confirmation 
date) 
• Cash Adjustment [[settlement]] Cycle - number of Days from Submission Date 
• Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle number of Days from Trade Date (T+X, where T=Trade Date)

Introduction 
This topic describes the [[settlement]] Override and the Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle process.  
Note:

How It Works 
Funds send [[NSCC]] a Security Profile for each security it trades through Fund/SERV. By specifying the security 
type as a Load, No-Load, or Money Market fund and Currency Code (USN and USD), [[NSCC]] can determine 
the fund's [[settlement]] cycle.

Funds have the [[Option]] of using an Alternate [[settlement]] Cycle for specific firms. The Security Profile specifies

Fund X specifies Firm Y as an alternate firm with a [[settlement]] cycle of Trade Date+1, any order from F irm Y for

[[settlement]] Date" for the transaction. [[settlement]] Override enables a firm to select when it wants the trade to 
settle without regard to the Fund's Security Profile instructions. 
The firm does this by providing 
• the anticipated [[settlement]] date in the Fund/SERV order, and  
• a reason code for invoking the Override. 
Note:  
The fund in turn has the [[Option]] of accepting the order as is (by confirming) or rejecting the order.

About Mutual Fund Post [[settlement]] Corrections and Cancellations 
Introduction  
National [[securities]] Clearing Corporation's ([[NSCC]]) Fund/SERV system provides [[NSCC]] members, which 
includes but is not limited to, [[Mutual Funds]], insurance [[Companies]], brokerage firms, dealers and trading partners 
participating in mutual fund services with an automated and centralized method for processing mutual fund 
orders, account registrations and centralized net [[settlement]]. 
Purpose  
The mutual fund post [[settlement]] system allows transactions to be cancelled or corrected after [[settlement]]. 
Fund/SERV currently has an extended correction process, which includes processing any gain or loss after a  
[[settlement]]. Because there is no simplified or standard way of processing the gain or loss within the system, 
many clients are not utilizing the automation. Therefore, [[NSCC]] has designed a simplified post -[[settlement]] 
process that does not calculate gain or loss. This is done outside of the automated process. 
Post-[[settlement]] Functional Requirements 
• Post-[[settlement]] trade transactions are identified when a firm supplies a "P" in the correction indicator after 
the control number field. 
• Post-[[settlement]] corrections or cancellations will be valid for a buy, sell, and an as-of buy or as-of sell. 
• Post-[[settlement]] corrections will amend the number of [[shares]], original price and account number. To keep 
the process simple, there are no post-[[settlement]] corrections for exchanges. Therefore, [[NSCC]] advises 
funds and firms to use firm-exits and as-of transactions. 
• Post-[[settlement]] cancellations are valid for exchanges and as-of exchanges. 
• Fund/SERV will not require additional Networking detail records for the post-[[settlement]] process.

Transfer Of Retirement Assets (ToRA) 
Purpose 
Transfer of Retirement Assets (ToRA) is an additional feature of Fund/SERV that will enable two mutual fund 
[[Companies]] to initiate, acknowledge, confirm, and settle the transfer of Individual Retirement Accounts (IRA) 
between the funds in a centralized and automated fashion. 
Manual Processing  
The current method of communication is for the receiving entity to notify the resigning entity of the request to 
initiate a transfer of assets or a rollover by the way of Letter of Acceptance (LOA). 
The LOA is analogous to a Transfer Initiation Form (TIF) used in ACATS. LOA's include such pertinent 
information as 
• the name of the [[Registered Owner]] 
• the assets, and 
• other relevant account information. 
 
In response, the resigning entity will then transfer the value of the assets directly to the receiving entity.  
This process currently relies upon paper communications to control and complete a Transfer of Retirement 
Assets. 
ToRA provides a standardized systematic method of executing these transfers that would eliminate most of the 
labor intensive and manual processes required in the current environment.

incorrect information. If an incorrect account number is entered, the Delivering Fund should pend the trade and 
review to ensure all other Critical Data Elements are correct. If all other Critical Data Elements are correct, the

should not cause a rejected trade. For true name mismatches, this code may be returned to give the Receiving 
Fund a clear idea of what action is needed to complete subsequent trades for the shareholder.  
706 – [[shares]] Held in Escrow  
Business Reason: [[shares]] in the account are held in Escrow, and there are not enough non- Escrow [[shares]] to 
complete the trade. Trades cannot be performed against Escrowed [[shares]].  
Example 1:  
Fund A requests a ToRA trade from Fund B for "all assets". Parts of the assets held at Fund B are in Escrow.  
Example 2:  
Fund A requests a ToRA trade from Fund B for $100.00. NAV = $10/share. The balance of the account at Fund B is 11 
[[shares]], but 2 of them are held in Escrow.  
Suggested Best Practice: The Delivering Fund should remove the Escrow from the account if possible, and 
then release the remaining requested funds to the Receiving Fund.  
 
707 – Uncollected [[shares]]  
Business Reason: Assets requested have not settled due to a recent trade. Assets must settle (become 
"collected") prior to transfer.  
Example:  
Fund A requests a ToRA trade from Fund B. The shareholder had an automatic investment from their bank into Fund B's 
account that occurred yesterday, which will not collect for five days.  
Suggested Best Practice: The Delivering Fund should review the trade and complete the transaction for the 
collected balance. The return file sent to the Receiving Fund should indicate there are remaining assets as an 
uncollected balance, which will prompt the Receiving Fund to wait 10 business days, and then re- submit a 
trade for the remaining assets.

708 – Amount Requested Not Available  
Business Reason: The Receiving Fund requested a specific dollar amount. That amount is not available in the 
account.  
Example 1:  
The shareholder instructs Fund A to transfer 13 [[shares]] from Fund B but does not have a statement available. Fund A 
requests the trade from Fund B through ToRA, but the actual share balance of the account is only 10 [[shares]].  
Example 2:  
Fund A reviews a statement for the account at Fund B, which shows a balance of $112. Fund A requests a ToRA trade 
from Fund B for $100, but due to market fluctuations, the value of the account is now only $98.  
Suggested Best Practice: The Receiving Fund should review the most recent statement to ensure that specific 
dollar or share values requested are available or make the request for "all assets". The Delivering Fund should 
pend the trade for review by a processor. If the amount requested and the available balance of the account are 
within a reasonable variance (usually due to market fluctuation between statement production and the date the 
request is made), the processor should release the amount of [[shares]] or dollars available in the account as 
though the trade requested was for "all assets". If the requested amount and the available account balance are 
grossly different, the trade should be rejected, and this code should be applied. Please note that this code 
should not be used to indicate Uncollected [[shares]] in an account.  
709 – Stop Code on Account  
Business Reason: The account requested has a "Stop Code" (restriction) on it. Some Funds place a restriction 
on an account due to required documents that have not been returned by the customer. Some Funds will place 
a restriction on an account if fraudulent activity is suspected, or if they have received report that the 
shareholder is deceased or in divorce proceedings. Some Funds will place a restriction on an account or put it 
into a "black-out period", when a retirement plan has moved from one Fund to another.  
Example:  
Fund A requests a ToRA trade from Fund B. Fund B shows a Stop Code on the account due to potential fraudulent activity 
reported by the shareholder two years ago.  
Suggested Best Practice: The Receiving Fund should verify the validity of the shareholder's address due to US 
PATRIOT Act requirements. Because of this, Stop Code rejects should not be sent for Returned Mail (RPO) or 
Stop Mail restrictions – the address at the Receiving Fund should be correct, even if the address at the 
Delivering Fund is not. For all other Stop Code types, the trade should be Hard Rejected.  
710 – Minimum Purchase Amount Not Met At Receiving Fund  
Business Reason: Some Funds have minimum purchase requirements that must be met in order for a 
transaction to be completed.  
Example:  
Fund A requests "all assets" from Fund B through ToRA. Fund A's minimum purchase amount is $50, but the account at 
Fund B holds only $23.  
Suggested Best Practice: Receiving Funds should review the shareholder's most recent statement to 
determine if fund minimums will be met prior to requesting funds through ToRA. This will reduce the number of 
Rejected ToRA trades.
