1. ABOUT THIS GUIDE 
The purpose of this guide is to provide customers with an understanding of how the trades received by the 
National [[securities]] Clearing Corporation ([[NSCC]]) Universal Trade Capture (UTC) system is  processed 
downstream and appear on the issuance of the Consolidated Trade Summary (CTS) . This guide presents 
information in one document relating to the functionality and rules relating to guarantees of trading [[obligations]] 
received by the National [[securities]] Clearing Corporation acknowledged on the issuance of the Consolidated 
Trade Summary (CTS). 
As the CTS was being discussed with various parties within [[DTCC]] it became apparent that no one document 
had the current view of data flow, validation points and downstream processing from point of receipt through 
[[settlement]] date. If in the viewpoint of the reader additional information is available to be added, please forward 
it to the author to be incorporated into the document. Consider this a dynamic source of information.

Trades are then classified into [[CNS]] eligible and non-[[CNS]] eligible transactions. [[CNS]] eligible transactions are 
sent to the Continuous Net [[settlement]] ([[CNS]]) system to be guaranteed by the Central Counterparty.

2. WHAT IS CONSOLIDATED TRADE SUMMARY (CTS)? 
The Consolidated Trade Summary (CTS) is a breakdown, by security and market, the net of all trade positions 
received by UTC prior to [[settlement]] date and includes a [[CNS]] and non-[[CNS]] section. 
The sources of data being submitted to the Universal Trade Capture (UTC) system to be sorted by [[CNS]] and 
non-[[CNS]] items and then compiled into the Consolidated Trade Summary that includes: Exchange trades, 
Trade Reporting Facilities (TRF) (such as, ACT, [[NYSE]] TRF, [[NASDAQ]] ORF), Electronic Communication 
Networks (ECN), Qualified Special Representatives (QSR), Correspondent Clearing (CORR), Exchange 
Traded Funds ([[ETF]]), ITP [[Prime Broker]], and CMU RTTM.  
Most of the trades received from the exchanges, TRFs, ECNs, QSRs, CORR, ETFs, and ITP [[Prime Broker]]  are 
for [[Equity]] [[securities]]. Some trades received by UTC have designations for specific UTC processing either 
marked on the individual trade or are known through a profile held at [[NSCC]]. The UTC processing includes: 
Universal Flips, [[CNS]] eligibility checks, when-issued processing for equities, Foreign Balance Order Netting, 
the [[ETF]] creation and redemption, and feeds to other downstream systems. 
A small number of corporate, municipal and UITs, (such as fixed income [[securities]]) are received locked in by a 
marketplace, not entered through CMU RTTM. However, all municipal bond trades should be submitted 
through the CMU RTTM matching platform at [[NSCC]]. The CMU RTTM system has unique processing to 
accommodate [[Bonds]] before they are passed to UTC.

4. CLEARANCE PROCESSING 
UTC receives the executed trades from the marketplaces and reports them back on the contract sheets. 
Trades are then organized initially by [[Member]], [[CUSIP]], [[settlement]] date, market code and contra. UTC 
summarizes the trade records the result is a trade summary record. The determination of [[CNS]] and non- [[CNS]] is 
also made. 
• [[CNS]] eligible records are netted to one buy and sell per [[CUSIP]] for each [[settlement]] date and sent to [[CNS]] 
for [[settlement]]. (The exception to this process is where the [[Broker]] submitted the original transaction as 
trade-for-trade [[settlement]].) 
• Non-[[CNS]] trade summary records are further evaluated using criteria to know whether the obligation: 
o Will settle trade-for-trade,  
o Be bilaterally netted or,  
o Included in the multilateral netting and allotting process. 
• Any resulting balance order is reported on the Consolidated Trade Summary (CTS).  
• Cash adjustments resulting from netting or repricing are reported with each balance order.

Universal Flip 
Some clients use a process known as Universal Flip where the executing [[Broker]] uses another [[Broker]] to 
clear/settle their [[obligations]] at [[DTC]]. Based on prior agreement from both parties, UTC automatically creates 
offsetting entries to net the “flip from” [[Broker]] and create an obligation for the “flip to” [[Broker]] for [[settlement]]. The 
CTS displays the universally flipped [[obligations]] with the market code Flip (FLP). All downstream processes 
after the flip from the submitting [[Broker]] to the settling [[Broker]] will be business as usual  
Change in [[CNS]] Eligibility 
Trades received by UTC are denoted on UTC output as [[CNS]] or non-[[CNS]] (whether near real-time through 
message or on the end of day trade contract files.) Internal to [[DTCC]], the risk management process also knows 
the trades that have been identified as [[CNS]] eligible. A regular way domestic trade settles according to the 
normal U.S. [[settlement]] cycle, which is currently 2 days. That same trade is included on CTS cycle 1 on the 
night of trade date. When the CTS is produced a re-validation occurs to verify that the trade(s) marked as [[CNS]] 
by UTC are still eligible. 
If the trade was labeled by UTC as [[CNS]] eligible, before the CTS is produced if eligibility of the security 
changes to non-[[CNS]], the CTS processes it as a non-[[CNS]] transaction, guaranteed, except for same day 
settling. Likewise, if the trade was identified by the CTS as non-[[CNS]] multilaterally netted, guaranteed then 
becomes [[CNS]] eligible before the issuance of the CTS, the change in eligibility would be indicated.  For 
example, if the trade date was 3/14/2022 and it was sent to UTC on that date with a [[settlement]] date of 
3/16/2022, the CTS would include it on the CTS cycle 1 the night of 3/14/2022. The classification on the CTS of 
[[CNS]] or non-[[CNS]] guaranteed or non-[[CNS]] not guaranteed is the indication of the obligation’s guarantee and 
method of [[settlement]].

5. NETTING TYPES 
This section contains information about netting types. 
[[CNS]] 
The [[NSCC]] central counterparty for clearance and [[settlement]] is called Continuous Net [[settlement]] ([[CNS]]). [[CNS]] 
is an automated [[book-entry]] accounting system that centralizes [[settlement]] and maintains an orderly flow of 
security and money balances. [[CNS]] settles trades from the various marketplaces and nets these transactions to 
one security position per [[Member]] per [[settlement]] day. The [[CNS]] system generates data that provide Members 
with a complete record of security and money positions and related information.  
While [[NSCC]] provides final [[settlement]] instructions to its Members each day, the payment and movement of 
[[securities]] ownership occurs at [[DTCC]]'s subsidiary, The [[Depository Trust Company]] ([[DTC]]). As [[DTCC]]'s 
depository, [[DTC]] also eliminates the movement of [[securities]] by providing book -entry deliveries, which transfer 
the ownership of [[securities]] electronically. 
The CTS reports [[CNS]] transactions with the netting type C and Net Reason Codes (001 –  003). See Net 
Reason Codes. 
Trade-for-Trade 
Multiple reasons exist for trades to be settled individually, (trade-for-trade), rather than aggregated or netted. 
Client preference, the client membership type, the security classification, availability for [[settlement]], and lack of 
a current price are all factors in the determination of trade-for-trade [[settlement]]. If the trade would be eligible for 
more than one of the trade-for-trade designations, the hierarchy would be as listed below, with a [[Broker]] 
submitted trade-for-trade taking the highest priority.  
The trade-for-trade netting type is T, the related Net Reason Codes are 040 – 054 and they are not 
guaranteed. See Net Reason Codes. 
[[Broker]] Submitted Trade-for-Trade 
If the trade is executed with the marketplace designated trade-for-trade (regardless of whether the domestic 
security is [[Equity]] or fixed income), the (SP) indicator is received by Universal Trade Capture (UTC) and 
reflected on the Consolidated Trade Summary (CTS) as Net Reason Code: 040 - Original domestic security 
trade was submitted with the request to process as trade-for-trade. See Net Reason Codes.

[[participant]] Ineligible for Netting 
[[NSCC]] has a special membership type for municipal comparison only members. This [[NSCC]] limited 
membership allows the client to submit municipal [[securities]] trades for matching and reporting by the CMU 
RTTM application. The trades matched as municipal comparison only trades are not sent to [[CNS]] or netted in

The municipal comparison only trades shown on the CTS as trade-for-trade as Net Reason Code: 051 - Either 
one or both parties to the trade are ineligible for [[CNS]] processing. It is processed as trade-for-trade, not

Trade Submission Ineligible for Netting 
Evaluation of the details of the trade submission may categorize it as a trade-for-trade netting type which is not 
guaranteed. Examples include: 
• Trades submitted as part of a municipal [[syndicate]] (original issuance) through CMU RTTM. 
• [[Equity]] trades with quantities in mixed, odd lots or baby [[Bonds]].  
• Non-[[CNS]] Bond trades where the pricing is sub-penny valued. 
• Non-[[CNS]] trades settling on the same day or submitted as of on or after [[settlement]] date. 
• Foreign [[securities]] trades settling in one day. 
Using the Cash, Next Day, or [[Seller]]’s [[Option]] UTC [[settlement]] types categorize the trade as a nonstandard 
[[settlement]]; therefore, the trade is ineligible for multilateral netting. 
[[securities]] Ineligible for Any Netting  
Certain [[securities]] are ineligible for netting. [[CNS]] eligible trades undergoing a corporate action and submitted on 
SD or SD – 1 at the end of the protect period is designated as trade-for-trade [[settlement]]. (Refer to Appendix: 
Net Reason Codes) 
Bilateral Netting 
Both domestic and foreign [[securities]] non-[[CNS]] trades that are not designated as trade-for-trade, however it fits 
other criteria based on the [[DTCC]] master file, such as: 
• Classifying the security as requiring special trade processing,  
• Non-[[CNS]] eligible security including municipals submitted by CMU RTTM are eligible for bilateral netting, 
or 
• Foreign [[securities]] settling in more than one day. 
The net of all buy and sell records for a given [[settlement]] date (typically) results in one receive order or deliver 
order. Trades included in the bilateral netting process are indicated on the CTS with netting type B and the Net 
Reason Codes of 020 – 026, denote that the [[obligations]] are non-[[CNS]] and not guaranteed. Any cash 
adjustment resulting from the bilateral netting is sent to [[DTC]] [[settlement]] Line Code 23 as a non-[[CNS]], not 
guaranteed Cash Adjustment.

Netting Types 14 
Multilateral Netting 
Only domestic [[Equity]] [[securities]] are eligible for multilateral netting. The netting process looks for trades with the 
same [[CUSIP]] and [[settlement]] date and summarizes by marketplace. The multilateral netting then encompasses 
all the transactions for a given [[settlement]] date per [[CUSIP]] and reprices all the [[obligations]] to a uniform 
[[settlement]] price using the Central Pricing Database current price before netting and allotting. The final results 
are shown as receive order and deliver order records.  
Trades included in the multilateral netting process are non-[[CNS]], guaranteed. Any cash adjustment resulting 
from the repricing and netting is sent to [[DTC]] [[settlement]] Line Code 02 as a non-[[CNS]], guaranteed Cash 
Adjustment. 
Pricing Non-[[CNS]], Guaranteed Transactions 
Rules for pricing used for non-[[CNS]], guaranteed transactions which are included in the multilateral netting 
process are as follows: 
• Closing price in the Central Pricing Database (CPD) at [[DTCC]] must be five days or newer to be used for 
repricing a non-[[CNS]], guaranteed obligation. If the price is stale or not available, the [[default]] pricing is 
used. 
• [[default]] pricing used for repricing equals: 
o $ 1.00 for [[Bonds]] 
o $   .01 for stocks (unless the [[Stock]] had a sup-penny price as the last known.) 
o If the price per share equates to a total amount of less than one cent, the value is set at $.01.

6. FOREIGN [[securities]] NETTING 
Both UTC and the CTS identify foreign [[securities]] trades by using the foreign [[securities]] value in the special 
trade processing indicator field of the [[DTCC]] [[securities]] master file. All [[securities]] are considered domestic, 
unless marked as a foreign security on the CTS. The foreign [[securities]] are reported on the CTS as non- [[CNS]], 
not guaranteed transactions. Foreign [[securities]] trades may be bilaterally netted but will not be repriced.  
[[settlement]] Occurs in More Than One Day 
Foreign [[securities]] trades are included on the CTS on the night of receipt and have an indicator of foreign = yes. 
If the [[settlement]] is scheduled to occur in more than one day, the trades may be bilaterally netted, (whereby the 
CTS reports the netting code as B, and the Net Reason Code would be 024. Any cash adjustment resulting 
from bilateral netting would be sent to [[DTC]] Cash [[settlement]] code 23. See Net Reason Codes . 
One Day [[settlement]] 
A one-day [[settlement]] received before 11:30 Eastern Time are reported in cycle 3. Foreign [[securities]] trades 
settling in one day are processed as trade-for-trade, reported on the next available CTS with netting code T 
and Net Reason Code of 041. See Net Reason Codes. 
Same Day [[settlement]] 
No same day [[settlement]] for foreign [[securities]] trades. The following table defining the foreign [[securities]] 
submission rules to UTC:

7. CASH ADJUSTMENTS 
A Balance Order, whether RO or [[DO]] can be reported with an associated Cash Adjustment.  
Receive Cash (RC) and Deliver Cash (DC) balance orders are produced in cases where there are no [[shares]] 
settling but there is a cash adjustment. 
The total of all Cash Adjustments for a given [[settlement]] date will equal the figure on the [[DTC]] [[settlement]] 
statement for each Cash Adjustment Code, whether 02 or 23. 
Guaranteed, Code 02 Cash Adjustments 
Cash Adjustment Code 02 is used for settling cash related to multilaterally netted guaranteed balance orders . It 
represents the difference between the reported contract amount and the value of the guaranteed balance order  
(which is determined by the uniform [[settlement]] price. For example, [[Member]] A buys 100 [[shares]] of XYZ [[Stock]] 
for $1,000 (price was $10 on trade) from [[Member]] B. At the time, the balance order is issued on CTS, the price 
of XYZ [[Stock]] is $11 per share. On [[settlement]] date: 
[[Member]] A would receive a code 02 adjustment of $100 credit + 
[[Member]] B would receive a code 02 adjustment of $100 debit - 
This $100 represents the mark to market difference between the contract value and current market value for 
each [[Member]]. 
Additionally, [[Member]] A would get a Receive Order (RO) balance order to receive 100 [[shares]] of XYZ from 
[[Member]] B vs. $1,100. [[Member]] B would get a Deliver Order ([[DO]]) balance order to deliver 100 [[shares]] of XYZ to 
[[Member]] A vs. $1,100. The $100 Cash Adjustment Code 02 would also be included on the balance order 
record.

Not Guaranteed, Code 23 Cash Adjustments 
Cash Adjustment Code 23 is used for bilaterally netted not guaranteed balance orders. It represents the 
difference in net money when the aggregated buy and sell [[obligations]] on trades in the same security with the 
same [[Broker]] results in no Receive Order (RO) or Deliver Order ([[DO]]) because the share quant ity nets flat (or 
0). 
Example: 
[[Member]] A has the following transactions with [[Member]] B.  
• Buy of 800 [[shares]] vs. 8,125  
• Sell of 800 [[shares]] vs. 8,000  
 
Because of netting, the following occurs: 
• [[Member]] A would have 0 [[shares]] vs. $125 debit 
o No Deliver Obligation ([[DO]]) or Receive Obligation (RO) is generated. 
o Balance Order indicator would be DC 
o [[NSCC]] would generate a Cash Adjustment Code 23 debit for $125 - 
• [[Member]] B would have 0 [[shares]] vs. $125 credit  
o No Deliver Obligation ([[DO]]) or Receive Obligation (RO) is generated. 
o Balance Order indicator would be RC 
o [[NSCC]] would generate a Cash Adjustment Code 23 credit for $125 + 
This example explains if no [[shares]] are moved, but a cash adjustment is needed.

• One of the following is sent to [[CNS]] (C): 
o Non-[[CNS]] multilateral net (M) 
o Non-[[CNS]] bilateral net (B) 
o Non-[[CNS]] trade-for-trade [[settlement]] (T) 
• Net Reason Code – based criteria of the [[participant]], security, and trade details the code was assigned. 
See Net Reason Codes. The trades are evaluated using the following hierarchy. 
o Was the trade submitted with special trade-for-trade processing? 
o Are any or both parties to the trade a Comparison only [[Member]] of [[NSCC]]? 
o Is the [[CNS]] eligible trade that was submitted as Cash or Next Day [[settlement]] undergoing a 
Reorganization Tender Offer or [[Dividend]] Distribution? 
o Is the trade part of a municipal [[syndicate]] takedown? 
o Is the trade quantity size defined as an odd lot or mixed lot?  
o Is the non-[[CNS]] trade a municipal security? 
o Does the price on the non-[[CNS]] bond trade calculate to be less than one US cent per bond unit? 
o Does the non-[[CNS]] trade have a security requiring special trade processing as identified on the 
security master file? Is the trade eligible for [[CNS]]? 
o Is the security identified as a foreign (non-U.S.)? 
o Was the trade submitted as non-standard [[settlement]] using the indicator for: Cash, [[Seller]]’s [[Option]], or 
Next Day [[settlement]] trade? 
o If the trade was submitted to Universal Trade Capture on [[settlement]] date after the “Cash cutoff”?  
• It only appears on the UTC Trade Contracts output, not the Consolidated Trade Summary. [[CNS]] Indicator 
– designate each record as [[CNS]] eligible or non-[[CNS]]. 
• Guarantee Indicator – denotes the trade record as guaranteed or not guaranteed. 
• [[CNS]] trades are guaranteed until settled.  
• Non-[[CNS]] trades are either guaranteed or not guaranteed. 
• Non-[[CNS]] guaranteed means the trade was included in multilateral netting.

trade.
