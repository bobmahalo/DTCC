INTRODUCTION 
Q What features [[DO]] the [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring (Limit Monitoring) tool include in its post-trade 
surveillance?  
A The Limit Monitoring tool includes transactions in equities, corporate and municipal [[Bonds]], and unit 
investment trust ([[UIT]]) transactions that are submitted to [[NSCC]] for clearing through its Uniform Trade 
Capture (UTC) system. 
Q Does Limit Monitoring receive and process incoming trades in batches? 
A No. The Limit Monitoring tool processes trades continuously throughout the day in near real -time. 
Q Does Limit Monitoring include correspondent-clearing transactions? 
A Yes. The Limit Monitoring tool includes transactions that were submitted to [[NSCC]] by special 
representatives (SR), such as CORR transactions, in addition to exchange and Qualified Special 
Representative (QSR) trades. 
Q What firms are required to subscribe to the Limit Monitoring tool under the proposed rule? 
A The Limit Monitoring tool is available to all [[NSCC]] members; however, the following groups are required 
to subscribe for the tool under the proposed rule: 
• Any [[NSCC]] full-service [[Member]] that clears for others. 
• Any [[NSCC]] full-service [[Member]] that submits transactions to [[NSCC]]’s trade capture system either as a 
QSR or SR, pursuant to Procedure IV (Special Representative Service). 
• Any [[NSCC]] full-service [[Member]] that has established a submitting firm (9a) and counterparty firm (9b) 
relationship to allow another [[NSCC]] [[Member]], either a QSR or SR, to submit locked in trade data on 
its behalf.

Q If my firm is required to subscribe to the Limit Monitoring tool, what actions must I take? 
A [[NSCC]] members that are required to subscribe to the Limit Monitoring tool should set up all appropriate 
risk entities and ensure that all appropriate risk entities are established going forward. This setup 
includes the timely maintenance of limit amounts and trade arrays. While members are not required, 
under [[NSCC]]’s proposed rule, to take any actions in response to an alert sent out by Limit Monitoring, 
they are expected to review alerts and make an independent, internal response, as appropriate. It is the 
responsibility of members to assess a situation that triggered an alert and respond to alerts, as deemed 
appropriate. 
Q My firm is mandated to use the Limit Monitoring tool based on only one of the above three 
mandate criteria. Is my firm mandated to set up risk entities that correspond to the mandate 
criteria? 
A No. The [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring rule only mandates using the tool, not how you use it. You determine the 
best way to use the tool to benefit your firm. 
Q When my trading activity breaches the established limit, does [[NSCC]] stop accepting trades? 
A No. [[NSCC]] does not stop accepting trades if an alert has been triggered. 
Q Is [[DTCC]] sending any information from the Limit Monitoring tool to third parties? 
A No. [[DTCC]] does not distribute any Limit Monitoring information to third parties. All information is sent only 
to the [[Member]] that controls the account or to the service provider a [[Member]] may designate to accept 
systemic alert messages. 
Q Is there a separate charge for using the Limit Monitoring tool that appears on my monthly bill? 
A No. There is no separate charge on [[NSCC]] members’ monthly bill. 
Q Is [[DTCC]] monitoring my Limit Monitoring activity? 
A [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring is a risk management tool for [[NSCC]] members to review their trading activity on a 
post-trade basis. [[DTCC]] can periodically confirms that [[NSCC]] members required to subscribe to the tool 
are using it. They can also, from time to time, review trade limits to ensure that they are set to 
reasonable levels. [[DTCC]] ensures that the Limit Monitoring tool is serving its intended purpose: not too 
low so alerts are triggered repeatedly during a course of a regular day and not too high,  so alerts are 
unlikely to be triggered at all.

ONBOARDING 
Q How [[DO]] I sign up for Limit Monitoring? 
A Complete the [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring Onboarding Form and specify the MRO files that you want to 
access. For information about setting up a super access coordinator and providing access to the tool, 
see the Access Coordinator Provisioning section of the [[DTCC]] Learning Center. 
Q Can I structure the Limit Monitoring tool to restrict access of certain risk entities to a select 
group of individuals? 
A No. The Limit Monitoring tool does not offer this feature. All provisioned users at your firm can view 
information pertaining to all your risk entities. 
Q How are alerts delivered? 
A Limit Monitoring provides several ways for you to inquire and to monitor your positions. If trading nears or 
exceeds the tolerance levels you established, Limit Monitoring notifies you of these events. View Limit 
Monitoring alerts in the following ways: 
• Within the application – From the Limit Monitoring tool, click View Alerts. Refresh the screens to 
see the most up-to-date information. 
• Email alerts – [[NSCC]] members using the Limit Monitoring tool can sign-up to receive email alerts for 
their firm. Unlike the on-screen alert notification, email alerts are automatically generated and 
delivered. Each firm can provide up to five email addresses as recipients of alerts that they can set 
up within the Contact Management section of the tool. Firms are asked to use only their firm 
business- email addresses for this purpose. 
• Message Queue (MQ) Alerts – Firms can opt-in to receive alerts through the MQ alert messages. 
Like email alerts, MQ message alerts area automatically generated and delivered. For more 
information about registering for MQ message alerts, see the [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring Onboarding 
Form and [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring MQ Alert Specs. 
Q Who can input email addresses to receive email alerts? 
A The email address input module is available on the home page of Limit Monitoring . The Limit Monitoring 
tool does not verify the accuracy of email addresses that are entered into its system. Only select users at 
your firm, who were previously provisioned by your SAC, can input email addresses. All users at your 
firm can view your firm’s complete email distribution list. 
Q [[DO]] I have to establish connectivity to receive alerts? 
A If your firm elects to receive MQ alerts, members are required to establish connectivity. 
Q Where can I find technical specifications for end-of-day reports and alert messages? 
A Access system specifications and record formats from the Limit Monitoring Record Layouts section of the 
[[DTCC]] Learning Center.

Q How many risk entities can my firm have? 
A Firms can have an indefinite number of risk entities. The number of risk entities is dictated by your firm’s 
business needs. 
Q [[DO]] I have to have a risk entity for all my trading activities? 
A Although the number of risk entities a firm sets up is not required under the proposed rule, [[NSCC]] 
members are encouraged to set-up risk entities for all trading activity to ensure that all trading is properly 
captured and monitored. 
Q Does [[DTCC]] provide historical data to help establish the net-notional limits? 
A [[NSCC]] members can access their RISK ENTITY NET NOTIONAL MRO files or reports. They provide the 
net-notional high for each day and for the month. Reported values are computed only for the existing risk 
entities from the inception time. To receive this output, complete the [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring Onboarding 
Form. 
USING THE LIMIT MONITORING TOOL 
Q How [[DO]] I access the Limit Monitoring tool? 
A After a firm has setup appropriate provisions for Limit Monitoring, it is accessible from the [[DTCC]] portal 
available at https://portal.[[DTCC]].com/. [[NSCC]] members can also access Limit Monitoring through 
https://ptrm.[[DTCC]].com/ptrm/PTRM/mainmenu.[[DO]] 
Q My firm has a couple of [[NSCC]] [[Member]] firm numbers. Can I see the activity of all of them and not 
just one? 
A You can register to view trading information pertaining to multiple clearing- firm numbers that are within 
the same [[Member]] family. The SAC for each of the clearing firms must provide this access. 
Q My correspondents also trade actively in [[Equity]] options to offset their cash -[[Equity]] positions. How 
can I view this activity in the Limit Monitoring tool? 
A The Limit Monitoring tool provides post trade surveillance of trade data that is submitted to [[NSCC]] 
through [[NSCC]]’s Uniform Trade Capture (UTC) system. As such, any data that is not submitted to [[NSCC]] 
for processing is not included in the Limit Monitoring trade monitoring. Members are encouraged to take 
the scope of the Limit Monitoring tool into consideration when setting trading limits and when analyzing 
and responding to alerts regarding those limits. 
Q Can I track my trading activity that is locked in by another [[Broker]]? 
A Yes. To create a trade array to monitor this activity, enter your firms [[NSCC]] [[Member]]  number, select 060 
– Over the Counter in the Submitting Market field, and the ensure the Submitting Firm (9a) field contains 
the [[NSCC]] [[Member]] number of the [[Broker]] that locks in your firm. The [[NSCC]] [[Member]] number is eight 
characters long.

Using the Limit Monitoring Tool 5 
Q What is the net notional amount? 
A In the Net Notional Amount field, enter the sum of the absolute values of net executed dollar volume for 
each security. For example, a risk entity with a net negative position in security X for $(-100,000) and a 
net positive position in security Y for $50,000, has a net notional exposure of $150,000. 
Q What is the difference between a risk entity and trade array? 
A A risk entity is a set of trading activity that a firm monitors together and assigns one trading limit to that 
activity. You can also group trades with common characteristics together into a trade array. You then 
assign a trade array to a risk entity. A risk entity can contain multiple trade arrays. 
Q How often can I change my risk entities? 
A You can change risk entities multiple times during the day in accordance with your changing business 
requirements. Some changes, such as the limit amount, description, and risk entity name become 
effective immediately. Other changes that involve adding, deleting, or modifying risk entities or 
associated trade arrays become effective the next business day. 
Q How many days does [[DTCC]] store my MRO reports? 
A MRO output reports are stored for only one day. 
Q What version of Internet Explorer [[DO]] I need for optimal interaction with the [[DTCC]] p ortal? 
A To get the most up-to-date information about browsers that are supported by [[DTCC]], see 
http://www.[[DTCC]].com/browsers. [[DTCC]] Client Support or your local IT support can assist you with browser 
questions. 
Q What is the Unassigned Risk Entity? 
A The unassigned risk entity captures the trade flow that is not match any of the existing user-defined risk 
entities. Limit Monitoring users can either set up a brand-new risk entity for this business or include it in 
an existing risk entity. As with any other risk entity, you must set the net notional limit for the Unassigned 
Risk Entity. 
Q How [[DO]] I identify what trading activity is hitting the Unassigned Risk Entity? 
A You can view the Unassigned Risk Entity content on a more granular level by using the drilling- down 
functionality within the tool. In addition, [[DTCC]] publishes daily the UNASSIGNED RISK ENTITY MRO 
files and reports. These aggregate the trading activity by executing [[Broker]] and provide the total number 
of trades for the day. To receive this output, complete the [[DTCC]] Limit Monitoring Onboarding Form, 
specifying the MRO files that you want to access. 
Q What is the [[DO]] Not Monitor indicator? 
A The [[DO]] Not Monitor indicator indicates to the Limit Monitoring tool not to generate any alerts for the risk 
entity. If you have selected the [[DO]] Not Monitor [[Option]], the net notional limit amount automatically 
defaults to 0, and you cannot edit the field.
