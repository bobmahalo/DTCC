About this Manual 7 
ABOUT THIS MANUAL 
This manual is intended to be a guide and reference manual for using the [[Underwriting]] (UW) SOURCE 
application.  
This manual familiarizes end-users with the UW SOURCE application, and explains the tasks that can be 
performed in [[Underwriting]] offerings, issues, and [[securities]]. It also provides descriptions for the fields in the UW 
SOURCE interface. Each part focuses on a specific area. 
• Offering Activity Processing - Part 1 
• Offering Activity Processing - Part 2 
• Dashboards, Work Plans and Work Items, and User Hierarchy - Part 3 
• Queries and Reports - Part 4 
• Eligibility Processing - Part 5 
Intended Audience 
This manual is for the users of UW SOURCE application, such as The [[Depository Trust Company]] ([[DTC]]) staff, 
underwriters, correspondents, transfer agents, [[issuer]], and counsel of [[issuer]], who need to perform various 
tasks to process deals at [[DTC]] using UW SOURCE.

Organization of Part 2 
Offering Activity Processing is organized into two sections. Part 1 contains five chapters that explain the [[BASIC]] 
tasks in working with UW SOURCE as well as tasks involved in creating and approving an Offering Activity in 
UW SOURCE. Part 2 of Offering Activity Processing explains the tasks related to enriching and working with 
deals. 
Chapter Description 
Chapter 1 - Enriching Deals Contains the procedures for finding an offering, issue, and security. It also 
describes the tasks associated with adding issues and [[securities]] in an 
existing deal. 
Chapter 2 - Attaching and 
Managing Documents 
Explains how offering documents can be uploaded and reviewed in UW 
SOURCE. 
Chapter 3 - Working with Deals Describes the tasks associated with maintaining deals such as 
deleting, postponing, canceling offerings, issues, and [[securities]]. 
Chapters 4 - Crediting 
[[participant]] Confirmation 
Describes the tasks performed by a clearing firm to confirm or reject 
a crediting [[participant]] relationship. 
Chapter 5 - Scrubbing Deal 
Information 
Describes the tasks performed for resolving potential matches at the 
offering, issue, and security level. It also describes the tasks 
associated with managing repair queues, such as creating and 
maintaining precedence rules.

Field Name Field Description 
• Limited Partnership 
A limited partnership consists of one or more general partners, who 
manage the business and assume legal debts and [[obligations]], and one or 
more limited partners who are liable only to the extent of their 
investments. These investors are not liable for legal action or debts the 
company may face. 
• American Depositary Receipt ([[ADR]]) 
A negotiable security issued by a US commercial bank that represents a 
specified number of [[shares]] in a foreign [[Stock]] that is traded in a US 
exchange. ADRs are denominated in US dollars. 
• [[Equity]] [[Derivatives]] 
 A hybrid security whose value is based on the performance of the 
underlying instrument. [[Equity]] Derivative trades in [[shares]]/ units. 
• Closed End Fund 
A type of fund having a limited number of [[shares]] listed on a [[Stock]] 
exchange. Closed funds redeem [[shares]] on a continuous basis. 
• Institutional Money Market Instrument (IMMI) 
A mutual fund whose investments are in high-yield money market 
instrumentssuch as federal [[securities]], CDs and commercial paper. Its 
intent is tomake such instruments, normally purchased in large 
denominations by [[Institutions]],available indirectly to individuals.  
• [[Equity]] Unit 
A security usually comprised of two other [[securities]] (the components) 
traded togather,  generally they consist of common [[Stock]] and a warrant. 
The unit can separate into the components immediately upon issuance or 
trade on its own until such time that the [[issuer]] decides to separate the unit 
into the components. The separation can be voluntary or mandatory. 
• Unit Investment Trust 
An investment company that offers a fixed, unmanaged portfolio, 
generally of stocks and [[Bonds]], as redeemable "units" to investors for a 
specific period of time. It is designed to provide capital appreciation 
and/or [[Dividend]] income. 
• Exchange Traded Funds 
A security that tracks an index, a commodity or a basket of assets like an 
index fund, but trades like a [[Stock]] on an exchange. ETFs experience 
price changes throughout the day as they are bought and sold.

Field Name Field Description 
• [[Warrants]] 
A warrant is a security that entitles its owner to buy a share of the issuing 
company's [[Stock]] at a predetermined price regardless of the [[Stock]]’s 
current market value.  [[Warrants]] are issued to enhance the future value of 
the [[Stock]] to the holder. 
• Depository [[shares]] 
A depository share represents ownership in the [[shares]] of a non-U.S. 
company that trades in U.S. financial markets. 
Like [[ADR]]'s Depository [[shares]] are created when [[shares]] of U.S. 
corporation are placed into a trust account or with a custodian bank.  The 
[[securities]] issued are termed to be Depository [[shares]] 
• Rights 
A security giving stockholders [[Entitlement]] to purchase new [[shares]] issued 
by [[The Corporation]] at a predetermined price (normally less than the 
current market price) in proportion to the number of [[shares]] already 
owned. Rights are issued only for a short period of time, after which they 
expire. 
• Corporate Bond 
A debt obligation or IOU issued by public or private corporation that 
obligates the [[issuer]] to pay the bondholder principal plus interest. 
• Corporate VRDO  
Corporate Variable Rate Demand [[obligations]] (VRDO) is floating rate 
instrument. They pay interest monthly or quarterly based on a floating rate 
that is reset daily or weekly based on an index of short-term corporate 
debt rates. 
• Certificates of Deposit (CD) 
CDs are time deposit products that are FDIC insured and have flexible 
maturity periods ranging from seven days to 14 years. 
• Debt Unit 
A security usually comprised of two other [[securities]] (the components) 
traded together; generally they consist of corporate bond and a warrant. 
The unit can separate into the components immediately upon issuance or 
trade on its own until such time that the [[issuer]] decides to separate the 
unit into the components. The separation can be voluntary or mandatory.

5. SCRUBBING DEAL INFORMATION 
The [[DTC]] Offering Activity scrubbing process enables UW SOURCE to determine whether to create or enrich a 
deal. This is accomplished by matching the [[DTC]] Offering Activity to a corresponding deal in the database. 
In order to create and maintain accurate deals, [[DTC]] Offering Activity is compared with the existing information 
and validated before creating or modifying the Offering. This process is referred to as [[DTC]] Offering Activity 
Scrubbing.    
[[DTC]] Offering Activity scrubbing results in the creation of a new deal, or enrichment or amendment of an 
existing deal, or dropping of the [[DTC]] Offering Activity. 
UW SOURCE allows underwriters and [[DTC]] staffs to send multiple [[DTC]] Offering Activities for a deal.  Each 
Offering Activity has a function code – either add (NEWM) or replace (REPL). Offering Activity that enriches an 
existing deal is marked as replace (REPL), and this activity results in an automatic match during the scrubbing 
process. 
Stages in the Scrubbing Process at [[DTC]] 
There are two stages in the [[DTC]] Offering Activity scrubbing process. They are: 
• Matching  
• Precedence evaluation 
The matching process compares the [[DTC]] Offering Activity with existing deals in the system.  
Precedence follows the matching process and subjects the [[DTC]] Offering Activity to the pre-defined systemic 
rules that determine whether the [[DTC]] Offering Activity can be used for updating the deal. Precedence 
determines who has the priority to enrich deal information. 
Matching Deal Information 
Each time a [[DTC]] Offering Activity is submitted, a matching is triggered. The matching process compares an 
offering, the issues within the offering, and all [[securities]] within the issues to determine the deal that needs to 
be enriched by the [[DTC]] Offering Activity.  
The matching process also determines the subsequent actions that need to be performed on the [[Underwriting]] 
[[DTC]] Offering Activity. Furthermore, it determines whether to create a new deal or enrich an existing deal in 
UW SOURCE.

Stages in the Matching Process 
The matching process takes place in two stages, namely, the issue level match, and security level match. 
This matching is done based on the matching criteria and rules defined in the system. The system performs the 
match at each level to determine the status of the [[DTC]] Offering Activity at the end of the matching process. 
Criteria for Matching 
The criteria for offering level matching include the [[issuer]]. The criteria for issue level matching include [[issuer]], 
Sub Issue Type, Series [[ID]] and Expected Closing Date . The criteria for security level matching include Primary 
Security Identification Code, and Security Identification Number. 
When an incoming [[DTC]] Offering Activity is matched with the existing deals in active state, the activity either 
results in an exact match, or potential match, or no match. 
Issue Level Matching 
The following four fields must be used as matching parameters for creating a new Offering/ updating an 
existing Offering: 
• [[issuer]] (by [[issuer]] [[ID]]) 
• Sub Issue Type 
• Series [[ID]]/ Issue Class 
• Expected Closing Date 
With the above mentioned matching parameters and matching methodology, only if all the four parameters are 
matched, then the Issue in that Offering Activity will be considered as matched with the Issue in the Offering. In 
such a case, the Issue in the Offering will be updated with the Issue in the Offering Activity. If the Issue is not 
matched, then a new Offering will be created. 
Only in case of Municipal Other Tax Exempt, a new Offering will be created even if all the 4 fields are matched. 
Offering Activity having at least one matched Issue will proceed for Security Level Matching. 
The Scrubbing Process will now proceed with matching the [[securities]] within the matched Issue. Only Primary 
Security [[ID]] with Agency Code will be considered for matching at Security Level. If the Security in the Offering 
Activity has Primary Security [[ID]] specified and matched with the Primary Security [[ID]] of the Security in the 
Offering, then the Security will be updated in the Offering.  
If the Security in the Offering Activity has Primary Security [[ID]] specified and not matched with the Primary 
Security [[ID]] of any of the Security in the matched Issue of the Offering, then the Security will be created in the 
Offering 
If the Security in the Offering Activity does not have Primary Security [[ID]] (instead Short Description is specified) 
or matched Offering does not have Primary Security [[ID]] (instead Short Description is specified), then the 
Offering Activity will be put in Repair for Security Level Potential Match.

Repair Queues 
As offering activities are subject to matching and precedence evaluation, issues keep arising in UW SOURCE. 
When this occurs, the system places the Offering Activity in the repair queue and creates a corresponding work 
item for [[DTC]] staff to resolve. 
Matching and precedence evaluation are sequential stages within UW SOURCE.   
Once the Offering Activity passes through the precedence evaluation stage, it enters the pre-update offering 
validation stage, which is the third and final stage in the Offering Activity scrubbing process.  
After the precedence rules are applied on the Offering Activity by UW SOURCE, the information in the Offering 
Activity is merged with the deal. The potentially revised deal needs to be validated before the Offering Activity 
actually modifies the Offering. Pre-update validation is the process of validating the potentially revised deal for 
integrity and consistency.

Reasons for Precedence Evaluation Work Items 
Once an Offering Activity has been successfully matched, UW SOURCE verifies to see if a precedence rule is 
available to determine the process by which the offering should be updated. 
If a rule is found, it compares the precedence fields in the Offering Activity and the offering. If there is a match, 
then the Offering Activity is passed to the pre-update offering validations stage.  
If a rule is not found or multiple rules with the same priority are found, then the Offering Activity is placed in the 
repair queue for manual intervention. 
Methods to Resolve Precedence Related Issues 
There are three circumstances under which an Offering Activity is placed in the repair queue due to 
precedence: 
• No applicable rule was found 
• The rule dictated that the item be placed in the repair queue 
• Multiple rules with the same priority existed 
To resolve issues surrounding the work items created, the analyst should contact their supervisor if the 
applicable rules [[DO]] not exist. 
For all other circumstances, the analyst may choose to retain the information maintained in the Offering of the 
deal, overwrite it with the information in the Offering Activity, or drop the Offering Activity altogether.

Resolving Precedence Rule Errors 
After passing the matching successfully, [[Underwriting]] Offering Activity falls into repair due to precedence rule 
discrepancy. Precedence Rule Discrepancy arises due to the following: 
• No Rule Found: No relevant precedence rule is found in the system.  
• Multiple Rules Found: More than one rule is applicable to the existing [[Underwriting]] Offering Activity.  
To resolve precedence rule errors: 
1. Search for the Offering Activity that is in Repair status. 
2. In the Offering Activity List section, select the desired Offering Activity and click Repair. 
In case of any discrepancy in precedence rule (No rule found), the Resolve Precedence Rule Error page is 
displayed. 
The [[DTC]] User can resolve the precedence rule error by either applying the created precedence rule again that 
is authorized by an internal user or by dropping the Offering Activity.

GLOSSARY 
Certificate  
A [[Stock]] certificate (also known as certificate of [[Stock]] or share certificate) is a legal document that certifies 
ownership of a specific number of [[Stock]] [[shares]] (or fractions thereof) in a corporation. 
Correspondent [[underwriter]]  
An [[underwriter]] that utilizes the services of a clearing firm to settle the transaction through [[DTC]]. The 
correspondent [[underwriter]] is not a [[Member]] of [[DTC]]. 
[[CUSIP]]  
The identification number created by the American Banking Associations Committee on Uniform Security 
Identification Procedures ([[CUSIP]]) to uniquely identify issuers and issues of [[securities]] and financial 
instruments. The [[CUSIP]] number consists of nine digits; the first six digits identify the [[issuer]] and have been 
assigned to issuers in alphabetic sequence, and the next two characters (alphabetic or numeric) identify the 
issue. The ninth digit is the check digit. 
Dissemination [[underwriter]]  
The Lead or managing [[underwriter]] with responsibility to submit new issue information for dissemination. The 
dissemination agent will generally be the Lead Manager, however, in cases where there are joint lead 
managers, the managers must decide which firm will submit to [[DTC]] for regulatory purposes. 
[[DTC]]  
Subsidiary of the Depository Trust and Clearing Corporation ([[DTCC]]).  It handles the [[settlement]] of trades in 
corporate and municipal [[securities]] and provides [[custody]] and [[Asset]] servicing for more than two million 
[[securities]] issues from the United States and 100 other countries and territories. 
Final Offering Document  
The final version of an offering document issued for corporations or municipalities.  It contains the complete 
and final information about the offering. 
Issue  
Stocks or [[Bonds]] sold by a corporation, municipality, or government entity. 
[[issuer]]  
A legal entity that develops and sells [[securities]] to finance its operations. 
Offering  
An offering is when a corporation, municipality or government entity elects to bring a new issue to the market in 
order to raise capital.
