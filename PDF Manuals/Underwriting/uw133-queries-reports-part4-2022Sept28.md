About this Manual 6 
ABOUT THIS MANUAL 
This manual is intended to be a guide and reference manual for using the [[Underwriting]] (UW) SOURCE 
application.  
This manual familiarizes end-users with the UW SOURCE application, and explains the tasks that can be 
performed in [[Underwriting]] offerings, issues, and [[securities]]. It also provides descriptions for the fields in the UW 
SOURCE interface. Each part focuses on a specific area. 
• Offering Activity Processing - Part 1
• Offering Activity Processing - Part 2
• Dashboards, Work Plans and Work Items, and User Hierarchy - Part 3
• Queries and Reports - Part 4
• Eligibility Processing - Part 5
Intended Audience 
This manual is for the users of UW SOURCE application, such as The [[Depository Trust Company]] ([[DTC]]) staff, 
underwriters, correspondents, transfer agents, [[issuer]], and counsel of [[issuer]], who need to perform various 
tasks to process deals at [[DTC]] using UW SOURCE.

Security Eligibility Query 
The Security Eligibility query displays the list of missing data elements that prevent a security from becoming 
trade or [[settlement]] eligible. 
Note: Only external users are entitled to execute this query. 
To view the missing data elements for trade and [[settlement]] eligibility: 
1. On the Dashboard- All page, in the Pending Items section, click Security Eligibility Query. 
The Security Eligibility Query page appears. 
2. In the Query Input Parameter section, enter the input parameters to run the query. 
3. Enter the [[DTC]]  Internal Security [[ID]] or the Security  [[ID]] of the security for which you want to identify the 
missing data elements. 
4. Select the Pending for Trade Eligible or Pending For [[settlement]] Eligible  checkbox to search for the 
missing trade eligible or [[settlement]] eligible fields respectively. 
5. Click Generate. 
The missing data elements are listed in the Security Pending Information section. 
 
Figure 4: Security Eligibility Query

Trade Eligibility Timeliness Query 
The Trade Eligibility Timeliness query enables you to view the list of issues with their eligibility timestamp and 
compare it to the date and time of formal award, and date and time of first trade execution. It also includes the 
on time or late information, and the exact time difference of time in hours and minutes. You can drill down to the 
security level and view the pending information for the security to become trade eligible. 
Note: [[DTC]] staff and underwriters are entitled to execute this query. 
To view the trade eligibility timeliness of an issue: 
1. In the Dashboard - All page, in the pending items section, click Trade Eligibility Timeliness Query. 
The Trade Eligibility Timeliness Query page appears. 
2. Specify the Dissemination [[underwriter]] Type and Dissemination [[underwriter]] [[ID]] for whom you want to identify 
the pending fields. 
Note: The search can also be filtered by [[issuer]], Primary Security [[ID]], [[DTC]] Internal Security [[ID]], Formal Award 
Date, Trade Eligibility Date, First Trade Execution Date, and On Time/Late. 
3. Click Search. 
In the Issue List section, the list of issues with their eligibility timestamp is displayed.

Glossary 48 
GLOSSARY 
Blanket Letter of Representations (BLOR) 
An agreement accepted by [[DTC]] from municipal and corporate issuers wherein the [[issuer]] agrees to comply with 
the requirements stated in the Operational Arrangements, as they may be amended from time to time. [[DTC]] 
requires an [[issuer]] to submit a BLOR only once for all its future municipal or corporate issues distributed through 
[[DTC]]. 
Certificate  
A [[Stock]] certificate (also known as a certificate of [[Stock]] or share certificate) is a legal document that certifies 
ownership of a specific number of [[Stock]] [[shares]] (or fractions thereof) in a corporation. 
Correspondent [[underwriter]]  
An [[underwriter]] that utilizes the services of a clearing firm to settle the transaction through [[DTC]]. The 
correspondent [[underwriter]] is not a [[Member]] of [[DTC]]. 
Committee on Uniform Security Identification Procedures ([[CUSIP]]) 
The identification number created by the American Banking Associations Committee on Uniform Security 
Identification Procedures ([[CUSIP]]) to uniquely identify issuers and issues of [[securities]] and financial instruments. 
The [[CUSIP]] number consists of nine digits; the first six digits identify the [[issuer]] and have been assigned to issuers 
in alphabetic sequence, and the next two characters (alphabetic or numeric) identify the issue. The ninth digit is 
the check digit. 
Dissemination [[underwriter]]  
The Lead or managing [[underwriter]] with responsibility to submit new issue information for dissemination. The 
dissemination agent will generally be the Lead Manager, however, in cases where there are joint lead 
managers, the managers must decide which firm will submit to [[DTC]] for regulatory purposes. 
[[Depository Trust Company]] ([[DTC]]) 
Subsidiary of the Depository Trust and Clearing Corporation ([[DTCC]]).  It handles the [[settlement]] of trades in 
corporate and municipal [[securities]] and provides [[custody]] and [[Asset]] servicing for more than two million 
[[securities]] issues from the United States and 100 other countries and territories. 
Final Offering Document  
The final version of an offering document issued for corporations or municipalities. It contains the complete and 
final information about the offering. 
[[issuer]] Letter of Representations (ILOR) 
An agreement accepted by [[DTC]] from municipal and corporate issuers wherein the [[issuer]] agrees to comply with 
the requirements stated in the Operational Arrangements, as they may be amended from time to time. [[DTC]] 
requires an [[issuer]] to submit an ILOR each time it submits a [[BEO]] or 144A issue for eligibility, if a BLOR is not 
already on file. 
Issue  
The stocks or [[Bonds]] sold by a corporation, municipality, or government entity.

[[issuer]]  
A legal entity that develops and sells [[securities]] to finance its operations. 
Offering  
An offering is when a corporation, municipality or government entity elects to bring a new issue to the market in 
order to raise capital. 
Offering Activity  
An Offering Activity is the information about an offering that is received from underwriters, agents, issuers, 
[[issuer]]’s counsel, service providers and [[DTC]]. 
Offering Documents  
A formal written offer to sell [[securities]] that sets forth the plan for the proposed business enterprise (or the facts 
concerning an existing one) that an [[investor]] needs in order to make an informed investment decision. 
[[participant]] [[underwriter]]  
A registered [[Member]] eligible for services at [[DTC]]. 
Paying Agent  
An entity, usually a bank or trust company, that is responsible for transmitting payments of interest and principal 
from an [[issuer]] to the security holder. 
[[Securities and Exchange Commission]] ([[SEC]]) 
A government commission that regulates the [[securities]] markets and protects investors.  
[[SEC]] - Registered Company  
An offering of [[securities]] that is filed with the [[Securities and Exchange Commission]] ([[SEC]]). 
Security  
A unit of an issue. 
[[transfer agent]]  
A financial institution that maintains records of investors, cancels, and issues certificates, and deals with other 
related problems. 
[[underwriter]]  
An [[intermediary]] between an [[issuer]] of a security and the investing public. An [[underwriter]] can either be [[DTC]] 
[[participant]] or Correspondent who would clear the [[Underwriting]] deal using the Crediting [[participant]].

The process by which new issues are introduced to the [[securities]] industry.
